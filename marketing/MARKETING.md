# SNACK TRACK — marketing

Everything about getting people to the app lives here, not in ROADMAP.md. The
roadmap is what gets *built*; this is what gets *said*. Same rule as the rest of
the repo: nothing is recorded as done that wasn't actually done.

**Read `../PRODUCT.md` first.** The three promises — simple, easy, no judgement —
bind the marketing exactly as hard as they bind the app. A post that would feel
bad to read on a bad day is as much a bug as a red calorie bar.

---

## The plan — Danny, 2026-09-17

**Organic first. No paid spend until there is consistent monthly profit.**
Word of mouth, content, and influencers carry it until then. Ads are a
conversation to have *after* the app is making money month over month, not a
lever to pull to get there.

### The engine: 90-day challenges

Run challenges (90 days, or whatever length fits) around eating and fitness, with
Danny's own daily tracking as the spine of the content:

- **The long-term content is Danny tracking every meal in SNACK TRACK, daily.**
  Not a campaign with an end date — the ongoing, boring, real thing. That is the
  product demo and it never runs out of episodes.
- **Challenges give it a shape.** A 90-day block has a start, a middle and an
  end, which is what makes people join at a specific moment instead of "someday".
- **Other content around it:** influencers, informational content, whatever else
  earns attention — the challenge is the format, not the whole feed.

### Channels

Heavy organic / word of mouth. Specific channels, cadence, and which platforms
are unscoped — see Open questions.

### When ads start

After **consistent monthly profit**. Not before. Revisit then with real CAC math
against the Phase 6 subscription price.

## The guardrail that decides every post

The no-judgement promise is the differentiator, and challenge content is exactly
where fitness brands throw it away. This is not a style note, it is the product:

- **No before/after framing, no shaming, no "cheat day", no "earn it".** Same
  banned vocabulary as the app (`PRODUCT.md` → No judgement).
- **A challenge is a consistency challenge, not a weight-loss competition.**
  Showing up and logging is the win condition. Losing, gaining, maintaining and
  plain curiosity all belong in the same challenge.
- **No leaderboards or user-vs-user comparison inside the app, ever.** That's on
  the "not building" list and a challenge is not a reason to revisit it. If a
  challenge needs a shared surface, it lives in the content, not the product.
- **Danny's own numbers are fair game** — it's his data and his face. Other
  people's aren't, and nobody gets held up as a cautionary tale.

Practical test, same as the app's: *would this feel bad to read on a day someone
already feels bad?* If yes, rewrite it.

## What has to exist before a challenge can run

Marketing can't outrun the build. A challenge drives installs, and installs land
on whatever is shipped that day. Minimum bar:

- **Phase 8 (Ship)** — the app has to be installable by someone who isn't Danny.
  Until then, audience-building is fine; "download it" is not.
- **Phase 6 (Paywall)** — if a challenge is meant to convert, there has to be
  something to convert *to*.
- The daily-tracking content, though, can start **now** and should. It doesn't
  need the App Store — it needs Danny logging every meal, which is Phase 2's
  outstanding "done when" anyway. The content and the dogfooding are the same
  activity.

## Open questions

Danny's call, unanswered as of 2026-09-17:

- Which platforms, and what posting cadence?
- Is the first challenge public-facing, or a private run to generate content
  before anyone is watching?
- Does a challenge need anything *in* the app (a start date, a shared cohort), or
  is it purely content? Default assumption: purely content, because the in-app
  version bumps straight into the no-comparison rule.
- Influencers — paid, gifted, or affiliate? Affiliate needs Phase 6 first.
- What does the challenge actually ask of someone who joins? "Log every day for
  90 days" is the obvious answer and is consistent with the guardrail.
